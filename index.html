A framework for engineering outcomes

Outcomes aren't persuaded into existence.
They're engineered.

The Outcome Model is a power-aware system for diagnosing why an outcome keeps reproducing itself — and for designing the specific, sequenced intervention that makes a different outcome irreversible.

The core model

One equation, five levers, one governing condition

Every outcome is treated as a state that evolves over time — shaped by five interacting variables, governed by power, and moved forward only when a credible trigger converts accumulated pressure into change.

Ot+1 = Ot + A[S, I, B, T, N ∣ P] × G(R) − D(V, X)
O — the outcome at a given time · S, I, B, T, N — Structure, Incentive, Bias, Technology, Narrative (and, in the model's fuller form, Sequence) · P — the governing power condition · R — the trigger that activates change · V, X — the veto, friction and shocks that erode it. The specific weighting and scoring logic behind each variable is part of the model's applied methodology and isn't published here.
The governing condition

Power isn't a quantity. It's a portfolio.

Most strategy treats power as "who has more of it." The Outcome Model treats power as five convertible forms that interact — the real question is never how much power exists, but which form of it actually converts into behavior change.

H

Hard

Coercive and legal force — sanctions, exclusion, permits, vetoes.

E

Exchange

Economic leverage — pricing, subsidy, market access, procurement.

A

Attractive

Soft power — culture, expertise, trust, voluntary emulation.

W

Network

Alliances, platforms, standards, and structural centrality.

L

Legitimacy

The multiplier — procedural fairness and normative acceptance that makes the other four durable.

What moves the system

Conditions aren't causes. Triggers are.

A structurally fragile system doesn't change on its own — it changes when a specific event reveals accumulated pressure and converts belief and behavior at the same time. Not every event qualifies. A decisive trigger needs all five conditions at once.

Salience

Hard for anyone to ignore.

Credibility

Evidence strong enough to actually shake existing belief.

Connectivity

Spreads through financial, political, media, or organizational networks.

Readiness

Enough pressure has already accumulated inside the system.

Amplification

Media, markets, or platforms magnify it further.

Don't confuse the four

Output, behavior, outcome, impact

A model that conflates these produces reports that look successful and change nothing. Each level asks a different question.

01

Output

What was produced — a report, a policy draft, a filed brief.

02

Behavior

What someone actually did differently as a result.

03

Outcome

What state of the system that behavior change produced.

04

Impact

What that state means at scale, over time.

Applying the model

A ten-gate sequence

Every engagement — regulatory, organizational, or policy — follows the same sequence. Each gate produces a distinct output and feeds the next.

01

Define the outcome

Name the state being pursued, precisely enough to know when it's reached.

02

Map the system and power

Who can preserve the current outcome, and who could make a different one possible.

03

Diagnose the current outcome

Score the five variables as they stand today, and why they hold.

04

Identify the critical constraint

Find the single bottleneck actually limiting the system, not the most visible one.

05

Apply Via Negativa

Remove what creates friction before adding anything new.

06

Design strategic options

Build a small set of real alternatives, not a single plan dressed up as inevitable.

07

Engineer the trigger

Distinguish triggers you must wait for from ones you can design and set in motion.

08

Execute and sequence

Order matters as much as the actions themselves — reordering is often the highest-leverage move available.

09

Feedback and adaptation

Update hypotheses, weights and resource allocation as the system responds — in real time, not after the fact.

10

Lock-in and PNR

Confirm the point where reversing the outcome costs more than maintaining it.

Operating maxims

Four rules the model runs on

Don't persuade when you can design.
Don't add when you can remove.
Don't confuse resources with power.
Don't stop at change — make the outcome durable.
A note on applying this

The equation is public. The engine isn't.

What's on this page is the model's structure — the variables, the governing logic, the sequence. What actually makes it work on a real problem is the scoring, weighting, and trigger-calibration logic behind it, and that only runs reliably in the author's own hands. If you're facing a specific policy, regulatory, or organizational problem and want the Outcome Model applied to it, that's not a self-service exercise — it's a direct engagement with the author.
